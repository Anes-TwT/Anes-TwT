Why the numbers are on the label

Most cold-weather marketing sells a feeling. A photograph of a mountain, a word like “arctic,” a jacket that has never been below freezing.

We test to comfort limit rather than survival limit — the temperature a person can stand still in for an hour without losing function, not the one they can survive for twenty minutes. It is a less flattering number. It is the one that matters when you are waiting for a bus at six in the morning.

If it is not on the label, we did not measure it.

Every rating on this page came off the same rig: still air, one hour, moving between 0 and 2 met. Fill power is measured after conditioning, not out of the bag. Weights are for a size M, garment only.
